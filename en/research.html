FRAMEWORK

How China's economy works

Policy signals travel through finance into local governments and industry, then out to the world through trade and the renminbi. Watch all five gears together.

Policy moves first

In China the policy signal comes before the price. A single sentence from the Central Economic Work Conference or the Two Sessions sets the direction of credit for a year.

Money flows through banks and local governments

State-bank lending and local governments' land finance create most investment. Know where the debt sits and you see where the risk sits.

Korea is connected through supply chains

Shifts in China's industrial policy pass straight through to Korean export items and supply chains. Every analysis ends with “implications for Korea”.

CSAI indicators

01

Policy Language Index

Compares the modifiers in Politburo, Central Economic Work Conference and PBOC policy texts from one meeting to the next, and scores the direction and strength of easing or tightening.

Source: original meeting statements and reports · Each meeting

02

Debt Location Map

Splits China's debt among central government, local governments, companies and households to see where it rises and falls. Location before totals.

Source: PBOC, Ministry of Finance, BIS · Quarterly

03

China through Korean Exports

Breaks Korea's exports to China into price and volume effects by product, to read the real change in Chinese demand.

Source: Korea Customs Service and trade ministry statistics · Ten-Day Note every ten days; price and volume split monthly

04

Provincial Strength Indicators

Combines fiscal self-sufficiency, reliance on land revenue and debt burden across 31 provinces to see the gaps between regions.

Source: provincial finance departments, NBS · Half-yearly

05

Tailwind–Headwind Index

Counts each week's policy signals as tailwinds or headwinds for Korean industries such as semiconductors, batteries, autos and shipping, and tracks the difference.

Source: CSAI policy signal log · Weekly

06

CSAI Judgment Record

Records and publishes whether each judgment we issued, with its as-of date and invalidation condition, proved right or wrong.

Source: CSAI judgment log · At each review date

07

Supply-chain Dependence Map

Tracks, item by item, Korea's import dependence on China for key inputs such as rare earths, graphite, urea and battery materials, alongside China's export controls.

Source: Korea Customs Service trade data, MOFCOM and GACC notices · Monthly

08

Frontier Industry Leadership

Tracks global shares in industries where China is among the world leaders, such as EVs, batteries, solar, shipbuilding, AI and robots, and the gap with Korea.

Source: GACC, industry associations, international agencies · Quarterly

CSAI GLOBAL SUPPLY CHAIN FOCUS · No. 01 · 2026.11

The Great Semiconductor Supply-chain Shift 2026

The chip map redrawn by US–China rivalry, and Korea's choices

The semiconductor supply chain is splitting from one global chain into two managed chains. Korea holds memory, the hardest link to replace, but relies on others for design software, equipment and materials. Drawing on 16 public sources, the report sets out who holds the chokepoints, who is pushed aside, and what Korea should do.

Contents: supply-chain map · chokepoints and concentration · competitiveness by stage · how US–China rivalry changes the chain · winners and losers · 2027–2030 scenarios · risks and opportunities for Korea · signals to watch
