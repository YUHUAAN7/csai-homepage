ABOUT CSAI

About CSAI

CSAI is a research institute that analyses China's macro economy and policy and connects that analysis to the decisions of Korean companies and institutions.

OverviewVision & MissionLeadershipOrganizationContact
OVERVIEW

Overview

The China Securities Administration Institute (CSAI) is an independent macroeconomic think tank that reads China's policies and markets through original documents and data, helping companies and institutions in Korea make better decisions. Research and analysis, the monthly China Economy Focus, business advisory, education and international cooperation run as one flow.

We test our judgments against the record we accumulate, and use the tested judgments to support the decisions of Korean companies and institutions.

POSITION

Where we stand: the boundary between China and Korea

China's policy15th Five-Year Plan · export controls · subsidies · regulation · monetary and fiscal policy
The boundary
1–2 quarters earlier
Korean companies' numbersExports · sourcing · prices · investment — the earliest statistics, on the 1st of each month
Translation
Korean decisionsSourcing · investment · pricing · people — management's China call
Export controls → semiconductorsBattery subsidies → batteriesShipping regulation → shipping

The hypothesis we test — China's policy shows up in Korean numbers one to two quarters before it shows up in Chinese statistics. We test it in every issue.

CSAI stands on the boundary where China's policy turns into Korean companies' profit and loss: a neutral platform, in Korean, built for corporate decisions, that grades its own judgments in public and carries contributions from Chinese scholars.

How we work

What we do

  • Macro, industry, policy and supply-chain analysis, and support for companies' strategic decisions
  • Research within public sources and lawful inquiry
  • Numbers with their sources; estimates labelled as estimates
  • Independent operation, with subscriptions and advisory as core income

What we do not do

  • Paid stock picks, price targets or trading strategies
  • Managing client assets, discretionary investment, or investment brokerage
  • Promoting access to non-public information
  • Due-diligence or expert-network style investigation, or collecting non-public data
  • Products that only monitor policy — nothing without judgment, data or access
  • Relying on a single sponsor or on government projects alone
STRATEGY

Four strategic directions

Four directions, taken in stages rather than all at once: research → advisory → education → future-finance research.

01

China macro and policy research

The monthly China Economy Focus (macro, industry, supply chains), Flash notes, quarterly deep dives and our own data

02

Business advisory and cross-border strategy

China exposure review, executive sessions, supply-chain, entry and exit strategy

03

Education: leadership and talent

China programme for executives, China-risk programme for financial institutions, AI and digital-finance training

04

Future-finance research: digital and climate

Digital yuan, stablecoins, security tokens (STO), carbon markets and green finance — analysis and reports only

FROM THE PRESIDENT

To ask about China is, in the end, to ask about Korea.

Behind the question “What will happen to China?” there is always another: “Then what should we do?” Our research exists to answer that second question.

Reading China accurately is reading the world economy, and where it ends must always be a decision for Korea.

An Yuhua, President, China Securities Administration Institute

Professor An Yuhua is an economist specialising in Chinese finance and capital markets. Born in Yanji, Jilin Province, China, she studied chemical engineering as an undergraduate, earned a master's degree in law at Yanbian University and a Ph.D. in business administration at Korea University, and taught at Yanbian University for ten years.

From 2008 to 2015 she was the research fellow for China at the Korea Capital Market Institute (KCMI), where she founded and led the periodical “China Financial Market Focus”. She was later a visiting fellow at the Korea Securities Depository and a professor of finance at Sungkyunkwan University's Graduate School of China (2016–2023). She has advised on policy as a member of the Policy Advisory Committee of Korea's Ministry of Foreign Affairs and an expert member of the Presidential Council on Intellectual Property, and served as a non-standing advisor to the China team of the law firm Yulchon.

She is now President of the China Securities Administration Institute (CSAI), advising Korean companies on investing in China and Chinese companies on investing in Korea and the United States, and since 2024 has also served as a chair professor (学科带头人) of fintech at a university of science and technology in Henan Province, China. She previously headed Yonsei University's executive programme in digital finance and virtual assets (to 2024) and advised the Samsung Global Management Forum (to 2025). She is best known for explaining the economies of China, Korea, the United States and the world in plain terms, through her YouTube channel and “The An Yuhua Show” on Yonhap Infomax.
